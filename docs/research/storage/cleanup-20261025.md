# Project quota audit and journal-preserving cleanup — 25 October 2026

The shared WORK project is close to its **file-count quota**, not its byte quota.
Our account does **not** account for most WORK usage. At the start of this audit,
project20305265 used489,714/500,000 entries (97.94%) and2.522/5 TiB (50.44%).
The account's filesystem-wide totals were83,042 entries and65.39 GiB: at most
16.96% of project entries and2.53% of its occupied bytes. These user totals are
not filtered by project ID, so they are upper bounds on this account's contribution
to this project, not an ownership census of other users' data.

The directory audit independently counted83,038 entries under the account's WORK
root at the start of cleanup. Of these,49,165 were in `.vscode` and
`.vscode-server`,19,131 in `projects/`, and14,169 in this repository (including
11,009 under `experiments/`). Thus this repository was about2.9% of occupied
project entries, not the main shared-quota consumer. Its roughly57 GiB includes
the retained study checkpoints. These are pre-cleanup directory measurements;
they need not exactly equal asynchronously sampled quota counters.

WORK (`/lustre/fswork`) and SCRATCH (`/lustre/fsn1`) are distinct filesystems,
verified by device IDs. Their quotas must not be pooled merely because the project
ID is the same. SCRATCH uses1.369/400 TiB (0.342%) and4,370,679/150,000,000 entries
(2.91%). The account owns approximately703 GiB and3.16 million entries there;
this is a substantial share of occupied SCRATCH, but nowhere near its allocation.
No SCRATCH dataset deletion is warranted to fix WORK's file-count problem.

Packed 204 untracked secondary files into a checksum-verified archive: 38 historical launch/submission/recovery helpers, 33 secondary images, 122 derived preflight SWC exports, six identical plan copies and five old reporting-smoke artifacts. Original datasets, checkpoints, all reports, linked report figures, corrected alignment panels, scientific implementation/tests, recent launchers and other worktrees remain intact. All 172 protected reports/dirty tracked files were verified unchanged before this documentation update. The archive preserves negative/failed experiment evidence rather than discarding it.

Archive and full path/hash manifest: `experiments/storage_cleanup/20261027/secondary-artifacts.zip` and `manifest.json`. Packed size: 22,746,668 bytes (27,925,544 bytes loose). The main gain is fewer loose files, not substantial disk space. Python bytecode removal is recorded separately in `cache-removal.json`. Existing compressed results remain in place. Experiment ZIP archives and scheduler logs, plus Markdown coordination locks, are now ignored by Git; reports, JSON/CSV evidence, source and tests remain visible. Nothing was staged or committed, and existing tracked modifications were not reset.

Historical launch commands require restoring their archived wrappers before rerunning. From the repository root:

```bash
# List archived paths and groups:
python3 experiments/storage_cleanup/20261027/restore.py
# Restore the historical launcher group:
python3 experiments/storage_cleanup/20261027/restore.py --group historical-launchers
# Or restore one specific file:
python3 experiments/storage_cleanup/20261027/restore.py cluster/jean_zay/fine_feature_architecture.slurm
```

Selective restoration was tested with a launcher and an image; hashes matched. Restore refuses to overwrite differing files. The archive is local and excluded from Git: retain it with research backups; committing its manifest alone does not preserve the archived bytes. Compact results and paper figures were deliberately retained even where untracked, because untracked does not mean disposable.
